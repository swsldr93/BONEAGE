Resurrection Methods
== t
Translated fragments outline a five-stage process used by the ancients to pull these creatures forward from the void. We admittedly do not know what the terms refer to, but have been able to gather a brief overview of the process:
== t
Extracting DNA: The process began by extracting and sequencing preserved “DNA” from the bone and tissue of the extinct subjects. Because time had degraded the remains of the subjects, the full DNA numbers of their closest living relatives were also recorded to serve as a blueprint. These close relatives are the source of variations we see in the populations of Sahul today.
== t
Computation: The ancient technicians had machines that calculated numbers based on what was found within the ancient remains. They could use this “bioinformatics” to infer the similarities between the extinct remains of the target animal and one of its living cousins. Together, they could get a complete “DNA” with filled in gaps.
== t
Special Cells: The technicians created "induced Pluripotent Stem Cells" from the living analogue species. These seem to be special cells without any coherent form, which the technicians could then put the full DNA into.
== t
Rewriting and Injection: The blank cells were then subjected to rewriting, what they called “CRISPR”, to make sure the traits of the ancestor came through. Then a process called “SCNT” was used to inject this new DNA into the beginnings of an egg of the relative animal inside its mother.
== t
Surrogacy: This engineered embryo was then chemically triggered to multiply, and put into a living surrogate host to be carried to term, birthing the ancient creature into the present age.
== t
Because this process relied on the DNA of living animals to patch the missing gaps of the dead, the resurrected creatures did not return in a singular, pure form.
== t
The resulting fauna express wild “phenotypic” variances based on the specific DNA analogue used to complete their sequence. The following are the variances that have been recorded so far.

Blue-Tongue
== lat
Tiliqua scincoides
== t
Found across eastern and northern coastal regions, woodlands, and open scrub. The largest member of the skink family, they are slow-moving, diurnal omnivores with heavy, broad bodies and short legs. They forage during the day for snails, insects, native fruits, and flowers, using their massive jaw muscles to crush hard shells. Because they lack the speed to outrun predators, they rely on bluff displays when cornered—flattening their bodies to appear larger, hissing loudly, and opening their mouths wide to flash their bright cobalt-blue tongues.
